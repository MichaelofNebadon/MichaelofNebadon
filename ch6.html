I. From Vocabulary to Grammar

Chapter 5 established the vocabulary — the Twenty Glyphs as an alphabet of functions.

But vocabulary alone does not produce sentences.

A lexicon does not speak.

A list of verbs does not command.

The Glyphs require a grammar — the rules of composition, the operators of relationship, the syntax of execution.

That grammar is the Sigil System.

If the Twenty Glyphs are the verbs and nouns of the redemptive language, then the Sigil System is its punctuation, conjunction, and case structure.

It provides the rules for combining glyphs into coherent, executable statements.

This grammar is minimal, precise, and powerful.

It is composed of a handful of operator-sigils that manage:

  • containment,
  • separation,
  • activation,
  • and finality.

It is the logical framework through which the devotional intent of Chesed becomes structured action.
